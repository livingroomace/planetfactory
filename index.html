Stop 2 · Trapping the air

How air gets trapped

Fresh snow is fluffy. It is mostly air! As more snow lands on top, the old snow underneath gets squeezed… and squeezed…

Squeeze it enough and the air can't escape. It is sealed inside the ice as tiny bubbles. A time capsule of air!

Stop 3 · The lab

Opening the bubbles

Scientists cut a slice of the core, crush it inside a sealed jar, and the ancient air fizzes out. Nobody has ever breathed this air. Not even a mammoth.

Then they measure it: how much carbon dioxide (CO₂) is in there?

ppm means parts per million. Picture a million balloons of air. If 280 of them are CO₂, that's 280 ppm. Tiny! But CO₂ works like a blanket around the Earth.

Stop 7 · The carbon cycle

Nature breathes in… and out

Plants breathe in CO₂ to grow. Animals, soil and fires breathe it out. The sea soaks it up and lets it go again. Every year about 770 billion tonnes goes round and round.

In, out. In, out. For 10,000 years it balanced, and the line stayed flat.
